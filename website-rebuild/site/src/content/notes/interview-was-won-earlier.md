---
title: The interview was won three weeks earlier
date: 2026-06-09
standfirst: The decisive moment in most difficult interviews happens long before the studio.
draft: false
---

When a difficult interview goes well, the studio performance gets the credit. It rarely deserves it.

The decisive moment usually happened weeks earlier, in a room with no cameras, when the spokesperson was made to defend the position against someone whose job was to break it. That is where the weak plank in the argument was found, and either fixed or abandoned. By the time the real interviewer found the same plank, it held.

Media training that starts with the performance has this backwards. Technique layered over a position the speaker half believes produces exactly what audiences have learned to distrust: fluency without conviction. The camera does not create that problem. It reveals it.

So the preparation that matters is argumentative, not theatrical. Find the question you are hoping nobody asks. Ask it first, in private, repeatedly, until the answer is true and the speaker can say it without flinching. The delivery work still matters, but it is the last coat of paint, not the structure.

If there is a question your organisation is hoping nobody asks, that is the preparation worth doing.
