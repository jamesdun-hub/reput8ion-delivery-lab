The most expensive sentence in issues management is "it has not broken yet".

It is usually said in a tone of reassurance, and it is usually accurate. The dispute is still in correspondence. The campaign group has not found its journalist. The regulator's consultation has not closed. Nothing is on fire.

But a long fuse is still a fuse, and the length is not protection. It is the one advantage you have and it is being spent, whether or not anyone is using it.

Every issue we have watched become a crisis had a period, sometimes a year or more, when it could have been shaped for the cost of a few decisions: a position agreed, a stakeholder spoken to early, a fix announced before it was demanded. The organisations that took those decisions rarely appear in case studies, because nothing happened. That was the point.

The question worth asking is not "has it broken yet". It is "what will we wish we had done in the month before it broke". Then do that now, while it is cheap.
