Two versions of the organisation are in circulation, one from the boardroom and one from the executive floor. Stakeholders have started to notice the difference.

## Why it goes wrong

The default move is to tighten message discipline, as if the problem were leakage. The divergence is almost never accidental. It reflects a real disagreement about risk, pace or direction that nobody has named, and message discipline papers over it until an AGM, an interview or a crisis pulls the paper off.

## What the engagement looks like

Counsel to the chair and the chief executive, sometimes separately at first. The disagreement is surfaced privately, resolved or genuinely parked, and only then translated into an agreed external position. Discreet by design and usually invisible from outside.

The client is left with one voice where there were two, and a record that the divergence was dealt with before it became the story.
